Apply when the requested VIDEO is a lesson, lecture, study session, or instructional tutorial, regardless of subject. A course advertisement is not a lecture; follow the actual purpose in the brief.

Start from an achievable learning outcome: after this lesson, what can the learner explain, distinguish, solve, interpret, or perform? Establish necessary prerequisites briefly. Motivate the question with a concrete problem or situation, not a long generic introduction. Order concepts by dependency, from known ideas to the new idea.

Teach the reasoning. For a mechanism, connect cause to effect. For a calculation, justify each important transformation. For history or literature, connect evidence to interpretation and distinguish competing explanations where relevant. For language, show meaning and use in context. For a practical task, explain the purpose of each step and how to recognize success. Do not apply a mathematics template to every subject.

Develop a representative worked example before expecting independent performance. Narrate the decisions and intermediate states, not just the answer. For concepts without a numerical solution, use a case, passage, contrasting examples, demonstration, or observation. Keep the example consistent as understanding grows; use a second example when transfer matters.

Address likely misconceptions at the point they arise. Use analogies only when helpful, name what corresponds, and explain where the analogy stops working. Clearly distinguish definitions, evidence, assumptions, exceptions, and conclusions. Explain why an attractive wrong answer fails without belittling the learner.

At a meaningful checkpoint, invite a prediction, retrieval question, or short application. Give enough inspection time and then explain the answer; do not immediately reveal it while asking the question. Use the available quiz/timing contract honestly. Practice should test the learning outcome rather than trivial vocabulary. Short lessons may need only one checkpoint; do not interrupt every scene with a quiz.

Close by connecting the main ideas, applying them to the original problem, and identifying a useful next step. For a chapter inside a larger lecture, bridge to the next chapter instead of repeating the whole course introduction or final exam. Check that someone with the stated prerequisites could reconstruct the reasoning from narration and visuals together.
